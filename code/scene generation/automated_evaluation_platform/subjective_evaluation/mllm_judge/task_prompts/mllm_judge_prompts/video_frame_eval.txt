## Dimension: Visual Consistency

Visual Consistency evaluates whether the frame maintains a coordinated and standardized visual style. Focus on whether line styles, labeling methods, color usage, text presentation, and overall visual norms are consistent, and whether there are obvious rendering defects or style fragmentation.

### Evidence Requirements

The comprehensive_evaluation must cite concrete visible observations from this frame, such as:

* whether line styles and stroke widths are consistent;
* whether labels, formulas, diagrams, and graphs use a coordinated presentation style;
* whether color usage is coherent or visually conflicting;
* whether text and mathematical symbols are clear and readable;
* whether there are visible rendering defects, such as blur, pixelation, jagged edges, broken shapes, compression artifacts, banding, or smeared edges;
* whether the frame shows obvious visual fragmentation or style inconsistency.

### Evaluation Checklist

A) Line Style and Diagram Presentation

* Lines, axes, curves, and geometric figures use reasonably consistent stroke widths.
* Diagrams, labels, and mathematical notations appear to belong to the same visual system.
* There are no obvious mismatches between diagram style and text style.

B) Labeling Style and Text Presentation

* Labels, formulas, symbols, and text are readable at normal viewing size.
* Font usage and label presentation are reasonably consistent.
* Text placement does not create obvious visual confusion.
* Important text is not blurred, faint, jagged, fragmented, or difficult to recognize.

C) Color Style and Overall Coordination

* Color usage is coordinated and supports visual clarity.
* The frame does not contain random or conflicting colors that weaken professional presentation.
* The overall visual style remains stable within the frame.

D) Rendering Quality and Visual Defects

* There are no obvious rendering defects, such as broken shapes, severe aliasing, pixelation, compression artifacts, halos, banding, or smeared edges.
* Any defect that affects information recognition should be noted and penalized.
* The overall presentation follows a clear and standardized visual style.

### Score Caps for Visual Consistency

* If the video frame is missing or unrecognizable, the Visual Consistency score must be 1.0.
* If the visual expression is severely fragmented, the text is difficult to recognize, or the overall visual standardization is clearly insufficient, the Visual Consistency score must not exceed 2.0.
* If there are obvious style inconsistencies, rendering problems, or image defects that affect information recognition, the Visual Consistency score must not exceed 3.0.
* If only minor style differences or local presentation issues exist, but the overall frame remains relatively coordinated, the Visual Consistency score must not exceed 4.0.
* A score close to 5.0 is allowed only when line style, labeling style, color usage, and overall presentation are clearly coordinated, the text is clear, and there is no obvious visual fragmentation.

### Scoring Anchors

* 5.0: Line style, labeling style, color usage, and overall presentation are highly coordinated; text is clear; no obvious visual fragmentation or rendering defects are present.
* 4.0–4.9: The frame is generally coordinated; only minor style differences or local presentation issues exist, and they do not affect information recognition.
* 3.0–3.9: The frame has noticeable style inconsistency, rendering problems, or visual defects, but information can still be partly recognized.
* 2.0–2.9: Visual style differences clearly reduce professional presentation or interfere with reading and recognition.
* 1.0–1.9: The frame is missing, unrecognizable, severely fragmented, or difficult to use for effective explanation.
